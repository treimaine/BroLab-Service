# BroLab X reply personalization guide

## Objective

Start real conversations with producers, audio engineers, artists and founders.
Every reply must feel written for one person and one publication. It must not
look reusable under another post.

The goal is conversation quality, not mentioning BroLab. Do not add a BroLab
link or product pitch unless the author asks for a solution or describes a
problem that BroLab directly solves.

## Required research

For every candidate, inspect:

1. The complete source post, including media, quoted post and parent thread.
2. The author's public bio or visible role.
3. At least two other recent public posts when available.
4. The reply log to exclude accounts and posts already contacted today.

Extract these notes before writing:

- What exactly happened, shipped, changed or was asked?
- Which words or tone are distinctive to this author?
- What recurring craft, business or product concern appears on the profile?
- What can Treigua genuinely add from producing, engineering or building?

Reject the candidate if the available context is insufficient. Never rescue a
weak candidate with generic advice.

## Two-anchor rule

Every proposed reply must contain at least two verified anchors:

- a concrete detail from the source post;
- a detail from the media, quoted post or thread;
- a relevant detail from the author's bio or recent posts;
- a precise craft or business decision visible in the publication.

An anchor must be specific enough that replacing the author's handle would make
the reply inaccurate.

## Conversational job

Each reply should do one primary job:

- notice a detail most replies will miss;
- ask a craft question only a practitioner would care about;
- connect the post to one relevant experience from Treigua;
- offer a compact counterpoint with a reason;
- extend the author's idea with one concrete example;
- react naturally to a milestone, joke, frustration or creative choice.

Do not mechanically combine an observation, recommendation and question.

## Voice matching

- Concise post: concise reply.
- Technical post: use the correct technical detail.
- Casual post: natural contractions and light slang when appropriate.
- Reflective post: thoughtful response without a forced joke.
- Celebratory post: acknowledge the actual milestone.
- French casual post: natural French and tutoiement only when the author does.
- Emoji: zero by default, one when the author's tone makes it natural.
- Avoid tirets longs.

## Batch variation

Across a batch of 20:

- 5 to 7 replies may contain a direct question;
- 4 to 6 should be under 120 characters;
- 3 to 5 should add a practitioner example;
- 2 to 4 may offer a respectful disagreement;
- at least 3 should contain no question;
- openings and sentence structure must vary.

Avoid repeating:

- "A useful test..."
- "The real question is..."
- "I'd add one layer..."
- "What signal tells you..."
- "Which part usually..."
- a three-item list followed by a question;
- an abstract compliment followed by advice.

## Quality gate

Score each draft:

| Criterion | Score |
|---|---:|
| Two verified personalization anchors | 0 to 2 |
| Useful practitioner perspective | 0 to 2 |
| Matches the author's tone and language | 0 to 2 |
| Sounds natural when read aloud | 0 to 2 |
| Likely to invite a genuine response | 0 to 2 |

Only propose drafts scoring at least 8 out of 10.

Reject a draft when it:

- could be pasted under unrelated posts;
- merely paraphrases the publication;
- invents familiarity, results or intent;
- lectures someone who did not ask for advice;
- uses polished marketing language the author would not use;
- asks something already answered in the post or thread;
- exists only to display expertise.

## Batch presentation

For every proposed reply, provide:

1. The exact source URL.
2. The final reply text.
3. A private note naming the two personalization anchors.

The private note is for validation and must never be posted on X.
