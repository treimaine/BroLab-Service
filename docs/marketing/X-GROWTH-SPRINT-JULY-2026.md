# BroLab X growth sprint — July 2026

## Objective

Recruit the first five active producer testers through conversations, not unsupported growth claims. Primary CTA: reply **store** or send a DM. Secondary CTA: visit the live storefront demo.

## Positioning

BroLab helps producers own the path from catalog to payout: one branded storefront for beats and services, automatic license delivery, and direct Stripe payments.

## Publishing rules

- One original post per day at 09:00 Europe/Paris.
- Spend 20 minutes before posting replying thoughtfully to five producers.
- Use one idea and one CTA per post. Do not stack product claims.
- Never claim customer counts, revenue, testimonials, or competitor fees without current evidence.
- Reply to every genuine response within two hours during the launch week.
- Track: qualified replies, DMs started, demo visits, sign-ups, activated stores, paid conversions.

## Seven-day campaign

### Day 1 — founder reset

> I spent too long building BroLab and not enough time talking to producers.
>
> That changes today.
>
> BroLab gives producers a storefront for beats + services, automatic licenses and direct Stripe payments.
>
> Looking for 5 producers to test it. Reply “store” and I’ll send the link.

Follow-up reply: `What do you currently use to sell — a marketplace, your own site, or DMs?`

### Day 2 — problem discovery

> Producers: what is the most annoying part of selling online right now?
>
> A. Bringing traffic
> B. Licensing/admin
> C. Getting paid
> D. Making the store look professional
>
> I’m building BroLab around the answers, not assumptions.

### Day 3 — useful checklist

> A beat store needs 5 things before it needs more features:
>
> 1. One clear genre promise
> 2. Fast previews
> 3. Simple license choices
> 4. A direct checkout
> 5. One obvious next step
>
> Which one is missing from yours?

### Day 4 — product workflow

> The BroLab sale flow is intentionally boring:
>
> Upload → publish → customer pays → files + PDF license delivered → payout goes through your Stripe.
>
> Less platform theatre. More time making music.

Attach a real product screen recording before publishing.

### Day 5 — build in public

> This week I removed fake-looking vanity stats and competitor comparisons from BroLab.
>
> Early products don’t need borrowed credibility. They need a clear promise, a working flow and founders who listen.
>
> What instantly makes you distrust a new creator tool?

### Day 6 — service revenue angle

> Beat sales aren’t the only offer a producer can own.
>
> Mixing. Mastering. Custom production. Consultations.
>
> BroLab puts services and beats in the same storefront, so one audience can buy more than one thing.

### Day 7 — beta invitation

> I’m onboarding 5 producers personally this week.
>
> We’ll set up your BroLab storefront together and I’ll use your feedback to improve the product.
>
> If you already have beats or a production service ready to sell, reply “beta”.

## Conversion follow-up

When someone replies with **store** or **beta**:

> Appreciate it. What do you sell today, and where do customers currently buy from you? I’ll send the most relevant BroLab setup link after that.

After their answer, send the demo or sign-up link with a specific reason it fits. Do not send a cold link before qualifying the need.
